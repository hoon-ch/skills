## The two dimensions

Diátaxis models the needs of a practitioner along two independent dimensions:

- **Action or cognition**: Does the reader need to do something, or know and
  understand something?
- **Acquisition or application**: Is the reader developing competence, or using
  existing competence in real work?

Their intersections produce four modes:

| Reader need | Action/cognition | Acquisition/application | Mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Learn through guided practice | Action | Acquisition | Tutorial |
| Complete a real task | Action | Application | How-to guide |
| Consult exact facts | Cognition | Application | Reference |
| Develop a mental model | Cognition | Acquisition | Explanation |

Do not classify by surface form alone. A numbered list can be a tutorial or a
how-to guide. An example can illustrate reference or support explanation. The
reader's relationship to the material determines the mode.

## Use the compass

Apply these questions in order:

1. What does the reader need at this moment: guided action or knowledge?
2. Are they acquiring competence or applying competence?
3. What obligation follows for the document?
4. Does the current content fulfill that obligation without another mode
   interrupting it?

Use the compass at three scales:

- **Sentence**: Identify a digression, hidden instruction, or misplaced fact.
- **Page**: Identify the page's single primary job and the reader state it serves.
- **Document set**: Check whether users can move between learning, work,
  consultation, and reflection without those needs being collapsed together.

## Diagnose blurred boundaries

### Tutorial versus how-to guide

Both direct action. Distinguish them by the reader's relationship to practice:

- A tutorial assumes the reader is learning and makes the author responsible
  for a safe, successful experience.
- A how-to guide assumes competence and helps the reader navigate a real task,
  including decisions and variation.

Test the page with this question: is success measured by a useful learning
experience, or by completing the reader's real-world goal?

### Reference versus explanation

Both provide knowledge. Distinguish them by when and why the reader consults it:

- Reference supplies authoritative facts needed during work.
- Explanation helps the reader step back, connect ideas, and understand why.

Test the page with this question: will the reader consult it while performing a
task, or study it to deepen a mental model?

## Treat the map as relationships, not navigation order

Readers can enter at any mode and move between modes as their needs change. Do
not require a fixed tutorial-to-how-to-to-reference-to-explanation journey.
Provide links based on the likely next need instead.
